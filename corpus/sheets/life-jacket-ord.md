## Specs
- Product type: Life jacket
- Grade: ORD (the masterlist does not define ORD)
- Sizes: kids (LIF-001-04A), adult (LIF-001-04B)

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A life jacket is worn on the body. It keeps the wearer's head above water. The masterlist does not define "ORD". It probably means ordinary. Confirm the difference between the ORD and HD jackets with CTI sales. The masterlist does not state the buoyancy, the wearer weight range, the material or any certification of CTI's jackets. Ask CTI sales before you choose a jacket for a person. In the Philippines, the Coast Guard has enforced a MARINA rule that passengers wear life jackets during a sea trip.

## Advantages
- A life jacket keeps a person afloat without effort, also when the person is tired or hurt.
- A worn life jacket works at once. The wearer does not need to find a float in the water.
- The kids and adult sizes let one supplier equip a family or a group.

## Cautions
- A life jacket does not replace supervision. U.S. Safe Kids Worldwide says that life jackets are not a substitute for close supervision of children.
- WHO says that the risk of drowning increases when children are near water without active adult supervision.
- A child needs a jacket that fits the child's weight. Safe Kids Worldwide says to make sure that the jacket is appropriate for the child's weight and intended water activity.
- Do not buy a jacket for a child to grow into. U.S. Oregon State Marine Board guidance says this.
- Do a fit test. Pull up on the jacket at the shoulders. If it hits the child's chin or ears, it may be too big.
- Fasten all straps and buckles. A life jacket must be snug but comfortable.
- Check the label for the wearer weight range before use. The masterlist does not state it.
- Wear the jacket at all times on or near the water. A jacket that is not worn cannot help.
