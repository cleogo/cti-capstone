## Specs
- Product type: Inflatable life jacket

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. An inflatable life jacket is flat and light until it inflates. A gas cylinder fills it. A manual jacket inflates when the wearer pulls a cord. An automatic jacket inflates when it goes under water. The masterlist does not state if CTI's jacket is manual or automatic, its buoyancy, its wearer weight range or any certification. Ask CTI sales.

## Advantages
- It is lighter and less bulky than a foam life jacket.
- It is cool and comfortable to wear in hot weather.
- After it inflates, it usually gives more buoyancy than most foam vests.

## Cautions
- It is not for children. U.S. Oregon State Marine Board guidance says that the U.S. Coast Guard does not approve inflatable life jackets for users under 16 years old.
- A child needs a foam jacket that fits the child's weight. U.S. Safe Kids Worldwide says to make sure that the jacket is appropriate for the child's weight and intended water activity.
- A life jacket does not replace supervision. Safe Kids Worldwide says that life jackets are not a substitute for close supervision of children.
- It is not recommended for non-swimmers. A foam jacket is a better choice for them.
- It gives no flotation if the inflation system fails, or if the wearer forgets to pull the cord of a manual jacket.
- It is not suitable for high-impact water sports, such as jet skiing or whitewater rafting.
- Test the inflation mechanism every year.
- Replace the gas cylinder when it expires and after each inflation.
