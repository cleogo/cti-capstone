## Specs
- Product type: Alarm bell (as named on the masterlist)
- Model numbers on the masterlist: ES-14A, ES-14B
- ES-14A voltage: 12 to 24 V
- ES-14B voltage: 220 V

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A fire alarm bell is a sounder. It gives a traditional bell tone when the fire alarm system operates. One maker sells fire alarm bells in a 24 V version and a 230 V version. These bells have a metal casing. They mount on a surface or semi-flush. UK guidance says sounders alert the occupants of a building.

## Advantages
- One masterlist line covers a low-voltage version and a 220 V version.
- A bell tone is a traditional fire alarm signal.

## Cautions
- The masterlist does not name the maker of the ES-14A or the ES-14B. A web search found no maker for these model numbers.
- Ask CTI sales for the datasheet before you rely on a sound level or a power rating.
- The two versions use different supply voltages. Confirm the supply voltage before you install the bell.
- U.S. OSHA says an employee alarm must be perceivable above the ambient noise by all employees.
- U.S. OSHA says the alarm must be distinctive and recognizable as a signal to evacuate.
- UK guidance says the warning signal and the sound level must suit the premises.
- A bell does not warn people who cannot hear it. UK guidance says visual or tactile alarms can be necessary in noisy areas or for people with hearing loss.
