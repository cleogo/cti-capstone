## Specs
- Product type: Rescue tube
- Sizes: small, big (both under code LIF-001-09)

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A rescue tube is a soft, flexible foam float. A line connects it to a harness that the lifeguard wears. The lifeguard can wrap the tube around the person and clip it closed, then tow the person to safety. Rescue tubes are common lifeguard equipment at pools and waterparks. The masterlist does not state the length, the buoyancy, the material or the difference between the small and big tubes. Ask CTI sales.

## Advantages
- The tube supports the weight of the person and the rescuer.
- When the tube is clipped around the person, the lifeguard can tow the person without losing the swim stroke.
- The soft, flexible tube is less hazardous in surf than a rigid rescue can.
- Canada's Lifesaving Society BC & Yukon calls the effective use of a rescue aid a safety precaution for lifeguards.

## Cautions
- It is equipment for trained rescuers. Canada's Lifesaving Society BC & Yukon says that lifeguards must be trained to rescue with and without an aid.
- Hold the tube where it is quickly accessible and under control, for example across the front of the waist.
- Do not let the line drag on the ground or cross in front of your legs.
- For a dive entry, drop the tube into the water first. Do not dive on top of the tube or its line.
- A rescue tube generally has less buoyancy than a rescue can.
- Inspect the tube, the line, the clips and the harness for damage before use.
