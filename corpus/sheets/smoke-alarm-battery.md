## Specs
- Product type: Smoke alarm or smoke detector (as named on the masterlist)
- Power on the masterlist: Battery (FIR-002-19 only)

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A battery smoke alarm needs no connection to the electrical system. U.S. USFA says that battery alarms use a disposable 9 V battery or a sealed 10-year lithium battery. U.S. CPSC says that battery-only alarms are the simplest type to install in older homes. Install an alarm on every level of the home, inside each bedroom and outside sleeping areas.

## Advantages
- It needs no house wiring and no electrician to install.
- You can add alarms to an existing home with no change to the wiring.

## Cautions
- The masterlist names of FIR-001-19, FIR-001-42 and FIR-001-43 do not state the power source. Confirm with CTI sales.
- The masterlist does not state the battery type or the sensor type of these units.
- U.S. CPSC says that smoke alarms most often fail because of missing, drained or disconnected batteries.
- A chirp from the alarm means a low battery. U.S. CPSC says to replace the battery immediately.
- U.S. USFA says to replace a 9 V battery at least once a year. Replace the alarm every 10 years.
- Do not remove the battery to stop a nuisance alarm. U.S. USFA says to open a window or press the hush button.
- A Philippine news report (GMA News) advises a test of the battery every month.
- UK (Scotland) home rules do not accept alarms with replaceable batteries. The sensors degrade over time.
