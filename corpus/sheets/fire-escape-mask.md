## Specs
- Product type: Fire escape mask

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A fire escape mask is also called a smoke hood or an escape hood. It fits over the head with a tight seal. The wearer breathes air through a filter that removes toxic gases. U.S. OSHA describes these devices as escape devices for adults, with no entry or re-entry into the area.

## Advantages
- The tight seal stops smoke from entering the hood.
- The filter removes toxic gases from the air that the wearer breathes.
- UK London Fire Brigade says that its unopened hoods need no maintenance.

## Cautions
- The masterlist name does not state if CTI's mask filters carbon monoxide. Ask CTI sales.
- A UK fire safety research group warns that some products sold as smoke hoods do not filter carbon monoxide.
- U.S. OSHA says that these hoods do not supply clean breathing air.
- They may not protect in air with too little oxygen. U.S. OSHA says this is a common result of fires.
- Use the hood only to escape. Do not let it delay a safe exit.
- The filter gives protection only for a limited time.
- Most hoods are for one use only. After you open one, you cannot seal it again.
- Many hoods are difficult to wear correctly. Practise with a training unit before an emergency.
- The U.S. voluntary standard ANSI/ISEA 110 asks for a laboratory-tested service life of 15 minutes for this type of device.
- U.S. OSHA advises that elderly people and people with breathing or heart conditions can have difficulty with a hood.
