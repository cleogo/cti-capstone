## Specs
- Product type: Standalone smoke detector (as named on the masterlist)
- Model number on the masterlist: 424
- Power on the masterlist: 220 V
- Maker: Not named on the masterlist

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A smoke detector on 220 V is a hardwired alarm. The electrical system of the building supplies its power. U.S. USFA says that a hardwired alarm usually has a backup battery. A standalone alarm is a single-station unit. It does not connect to other alarms, so only that unit sounds. U.S. CPSC says that new homes typically have hardwired alarms.

## Advantages
- The electrical system supplies the main power, so the alarm does not depend only on a battery.
- U.S. CPSC says that a hardwired alarm with a backup battery gives protection also during a power outage.

## Cautions
- The masterlist does not name the maker of the 424. A web search found no single maker for this model number.
- The masterlist name does not state a backup battery or the sensor type. Ask CTI sales for these details.
- U.S. USFA says that only qualified electricians should install hardwired alarms.
- UK (Scotland) guidance also says that a qualified electrician must fit mains-wired alarms.
- U.S. USFA says to replace the backup battery at least once a year. Replace the alarm every 10 years.
- A standalone alarm does not alert people in other rooms. U.S. CPSC advises interconnected alarms.
