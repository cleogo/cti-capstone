## Specs
- Product type: Rescue can
- Sizes: small (LIF-001-08A), big (LIF-001-08B)

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A rescue can is also called a rescue buoy or a torpedo buoy. It is a rigid float with handles. A rope connects it to a strap that the lifeguard wears. The lifeguard swims to the person with the can trailing behind. The person holds the handles, and the lifeguard tows the person to safety. Rescue cans are standard equipment for beach and other open-water lifeguards. The masterlist does not state the buoyancy, the material, the dimensions or the difference between the small and big cans. Ask CTI sales.

## Advantages
- The can supports the weight of the person and helps the rescuer stay afloat. This makes the rescue safer for the rescuer.
- A rescue buoy generally has more buoyancy than a rescue tube, so it can help more than one person.
- The handles give the person a firm grip.

## Cautions
- It is equipment for trained rescuers. Using it properly needs practice. Lifeguard training is recommended.
- The can is rigid. In surf, it can hit and hurt people.
- The person must be able to hold on to the handles.
- Currents, waves and wind can change rapidly. Do not go into the water to rescue a person unless you are trained.
- Inspect the can, the rope and the strap for cracks and wear before use.
