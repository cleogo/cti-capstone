## Specs
- Product type: Class K fire extinguisher (as named on the masterlist)
- Sizes on the masterlist: 10 lb, 20 lb

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of extinguisher, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. Class K fires involve vegetable oils, animal oils and fats in cooking appliances. Class K extinguishers are often found in commercial kitchens. Many are wet chemical units that spray a fine mist, and some dry chemical units use potassium bicarbonate. One maker's Class K unit uses a potassium acetate base agent.

## Advantages
- A wet chemical agent seals the fuel to stop vapors from igniting. It also cools the fire.
- Range hoods and Class B extinguishers cannot always put out a deep fryer fire. NFPA 10 added the Class K rating for this hazard.
- One maker designs its Class K units to supplement automatic kitchen suppression systems.

## Cautions
- The masterlist name does not state the agent in CTI's unit.
- The agents in many Class K extinguishers conduct electricity. Shut off power to the appliance before use.
- U.S. OSHA says to use a Class K extinguisher only after the built-in hood suppression system activates.
- U.S. OSHA guidance sets a maximum travel distance of 30 feet to a Class K extinguisher.
- Inspect, test and maintain Class K extinguishers every year.
