## Specs
- Product type: Fire call point (as named on the masterlist)
- Operation: Manual

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A fire call point is a manual device on a fire alarm system. A person who sees a fire operates it to start the alarm. Some call points use a glass element that breaks. Others use a resettable element. One maker's call point accepts both types of element. UK guidance puts call points at exit doors. It says they must be conspicuous and no higher than 1.4 m from the floor. A lower position can suit wheelchair users.

## Advantages
- Any occupant can start the fire alarm by hand.
- On one maker's call point, a yellow strip shows that the element has operated.
- On the same call point, a test key lets a technician test the switch without breaking the glass.

## Cautions
- A call point is one part of a fire alarm system. It needs a compatible panel or sounder to warn people.
- UK guidance says occupants must not travel more than 45 m to the nearest call point.
- UK guidance recommends hinged covers on call points in public areas. Covers help to prevent accidental use.
- U.S. OSHA says manual alarm devices must be unobstructed, conspicuous and readily accessible.
- U.S. OSHA requires a test every two months for an alarm system that is not supervised. Each test must use a different device.
- The masterlist name does not state the element type, the voltage or the weather rating of CTI's unit.
