## Specs
- Product type: Life ring
- Sizes: small (LIF-001-01A), big (LIF-001-01B)

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A life ring is also called a ring buoy or a lifebuoy. A rescuer on a boat, a dock or the shore throws it to a person in the water. The person holds on to it and stays afloat. The U.S. Coast Guard classes a ring buoy as a Type IV throwable device: it is designed to be thrown, not worn. The masterlist does not state the buoyancy, the diameter, the material, any approval of CTI's rings, or if a throw line is included. Ask CTI sales.

## Advantages
- The rescuer can help a person in the water without going into the water.
- A ring with a throw line lets the rescuer pull the person back to the edge.
- It is ready to use at once. It needs no inflation.

## Cautions
- U.S. boating guidance says that a throwable device is not for a person who cannot hold on to it, and not for rough water.
- It is not a life jacket. Do not use it in place of one.
- Before you throw, stand on the free end of the throw line, so that the line does not go into the water with the ring.
- Throw underhand. Aim the ring to land just past the person, so that the line falls near the person's shoulder.
- UK Cheshire Fire and Rescue Service advises: never go into the water yourself. If you feel yourself being pulled in, let go.
- Call the emergency services as soon as you see a person in difficulty.
- Keep the ring where people can get it immediately. U.S. rules say a throwable device must be immediately available.
- Inspect the ring and the line for cracks, tears and wear.
