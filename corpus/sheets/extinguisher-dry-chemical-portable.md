## Specs
- Product type: Portable fire extinguisher
- Agent: Dry chemical
- Sizes on the masterlist: 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15 and 20 lb (15 lb in the FIR-001 range only)
- Mounting: FIR-002 units are wall type, with a free wall bracket (1, 2, 3, 5 lb) or wall hook (10, 20 lb). FIR-001 names do not state a mounting.

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of extinguisher, not the datasheet of CTI's unit.
Dry chemical is one of the common extinguishing agents. The label names the
fire classes a unit covers, such as "B-C" or "A-B-C". ABC dry chemical is
monoammonium phosphate. It coats Class A fuels and breaks the chain reaction of
Class B fires. The masterlist name does not say which dry chemical the unit
holds, so read the label for its fire classes. To use it, pull the pin, aim low
at the base of the fire, squeeze the lever and sweep.

## Advantages
- If the label says ABC, the unit covers ordinary combustibles, flammable liquids and energized electrical equipment.
- ABC dry chemical, as one maker states, does not conduct electricity back to the operator.
- ABC units suit offices, schools, warehouses, retail areas and hotels.
- The range runs from 1 lb to 20 lb. The USFA advises the largest unit a user can safely operate.

## Cautions
- Use an extinguisher only on a small fire contained in one object. Make sure you have a clear escape route.
- Young children and older adults should not use fire extinguishers. If in doubt, leave the building and call for help.
- Check that nothing blocks the unit. Check the gauge, and look for dents, rust or a damaged hose or nozzle.
- You can recharge some units. Others need replacement when the pressure is too low.
- In workplaces, U.S. OSHA requires a monthly visual inspection and an annual maintenance check.
- U.S. OSHA requires a hydrostatic test every 12 years for stored-pressure dry chemical units with mild steel shells. Owners must also empty and service these units every 6 years.
