## Specs
- Product type: First aid kit
- Piece count: 176 pcs

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A first aid kit holds supplies for first aid: the care given for an injury or sudden illness before medical treatment is available. The masterlist states only that this kit has 176 pieces. It does not state the contents of CTI's kit, or if the kit meets a standard such as U.S. ANSI Z308.1 or UK BS 8599. Ask CTI sales for the content list.

## Advantages
- It keeps first aid supplies together in one place.
- It can be kept in a home, a workplace or a vehicle.

## Cautions
- Compare the contents with your needs before you rely on the kit. U.S. OSHA advises employers to assess the needs of their worksite periodically and to add to the kit as needed.
- U.S. OSHA cites ANSI Z308.1 as an example of the minimum contents of a generic workplace kit. The masterlist does not state that CTI's kit meets ANSI Z308.1.
- UK HSE says that a minimum kit for low-risk work might contain a first aid guidance leaflet, individually wrapped sterile plasters of assorted sizes, sterile eye pads, individually wrapped triangular bandages, safety pins, large and medium sterile unmedicated wound dressings, and disposable gloves.
- For large or multiple operations at one site, U.S. OSHA advises employers to decide if they need more kits.
- U.S. OSHA says that first aid supplies must be readily accessible. Make sure that everybody knows where the kit is kept.
- Check the kit regularly. St John Ambulance Australia advises to check that packets are sealed, that no item is past its expiry date, and that used items are replaced.
- A kit does not replace first aid training. U.S. OSHA requires a person trained in first aid at a worksite that is not near an infirmary, clinic or hospital.
- St John Ambulance Australia advises each person to carry their own medicines.
