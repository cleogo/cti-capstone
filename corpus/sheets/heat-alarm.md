## Specs
- Product type: Heat alarm (as named on the masterlist)
- Detects: Heat, not smoke

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A heat alarm reacts to heat, not to smoke. It gives a warning when the air temperature around it increases by a large amount. Some heat alarms operate when the air reaches a fixed temperature. Some also respond to a fast rise in temperature. Heat alarms are for kitchens and garages. In these areas, smoke alarms often give false alarms. Install a heat alarm on the ceiling, away from sources of heat.

## Advantages
- Cooking fumes and steam do not easily cause a heat alarm to sound.
- U.S. USFA says dust, fumes, insects, temperature changes and humidity changes do not affect heat alarms.
- Many heat alarms can interconnect with smoke alarms. Then all the alarms sound together.
- UK guidance says heat detectors have a good record for few false alarms.

## Cautions
- A heat alarm does not detect smoke. UK guidance says not to use one where smoke detection is necessary, for example in escape routes.
- A heat alarm does not replace smoke alarms. One UK maker recommends interconnected smoke and heat alarms across the property.
- Scottish law requires one heat alarm in the kitchen of every home. All smoke and heat alarms go on the ceiling and are interlinked.
- U.S. USFA advises a hard-wired heat alarm with battery backup. A qualified electrician must install the hard-wired unit.
- U.S. USFA says not to install a heat alarm near fluorescent lights.
- The masterlist name does not state the power source, the sensing method or the alarm temperature of CTI's unit.
