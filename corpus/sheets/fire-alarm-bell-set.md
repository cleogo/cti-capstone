## Specs
- Product type: Fire alarm bell with manual call point, sold as a set (as named on the masterlist)
- Bell sizes on the masterlist: 6 in, 8 in, 10 in
- Voltage: 220 V
- Activation: Manual push button (manual call point)

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. The set joins a fire alarm bell to a manual call point. A person who sees a fire operates the call point. Then the bell sounds to alert the occupants. A fire alarm bell gives a traditional bell tone. UK guidance says a building needs sounders and call points when a shout is not heard throughout. UK guidance puts call points at exit doors, no higher than 1.4 m from the floor.

## Advantages
- One set gives both the call point that starts the alarm and the bell that sounds it.
- The masterlist has three bell sizes.

## Cautions
- The masterlist name lists no automatic detector. The bell sounds only when a person operates the call point.
- The masterlist name does not state the sound level of each bell size.
- UK guidance says occupants must not travel more than 45 m to the nearest call point.
- U.S. OSHA says manual alarm devices must be unobstructed, conspicuous and readily accessible.
- U.S. OSHA says the alarm must be perceivable above the ambient noise by all employees.
- U.S. OSHA requires a test every two months for an employee alarm system that is not supervised.
- U.S. OSHA says only persons trained in the system can service, maintain and test it.
- UK guidance says visual or tactile alarms can be necessary in noisy areas or for people with hearing loss.
