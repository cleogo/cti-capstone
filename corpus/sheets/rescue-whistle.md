## Specs
- Product type: Rescue whistle (as named on the masterlist)
- Maker: Not named on the masterlist

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A rescue whistle lets a person call for help with less effort than shouting. U.S. Ready.gov lists a whistle to signal for help in a basic disaster supplies kit. If you are trapped after an earthquake, U.S. Ready.gov says to cover your mouth and use a whistle instead of shouting. Be Ready Utah, a U.S. state preparedness site, says that three blasts of a whistle are a universal signal of distress. The masterlist states only the name. It does not state the sound level or the material of CTI's whistle, or if it has a pea. Ask CTI sales.

## Advantages
- Be Ready Utah says that the sound of a whistle carries farther than a voice and takes less effort.
- It needs no battery or power.
- It is small and light. You can attach it to keys, a bag or a life jacket.

## Cautions
- The masterlist does not state if CTI's whistle has a pea. Ask CTI sales.
- One whistle maker (Fox 40) says that a pea whistle can jam from moisture, dirt or ice, and then gives no sound.
- Keep the whistle where you can reach it fast, for example on a cord or in an emergency kit.
- Do not blow a whistle close to the ear of another person.
