## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A smoke alarm senses smoke and makes a loud alarm sound. The two main sensor types are ionization and photoelectric. U.S. CPSC says that an ionization sensor responds more quickly to flaming fires. A photoelectric sensor responds more quickly to smoldering fires. U.S. CPSC advises a smoke alarm on every level of the home, inside each bedroom and outside sleeping areas. Install it in the center of a ceiling, or on a wall near the ceiling.

## Advantages
- A smoke alarm operates 24 hours a day. It gives early warning of a fire, also when people sleep.
- U.S. CPSC says that working smoke alarms can reduce the risk of death in a home fire by almost half.

## Cautions
- The masterlist does not name the maker of the SD-604PC. A web search found no single maker for this model number.
- The masterlist name does not state the sensor type or the power source. Ask CTI sales for these details.
- U.S. USFA says to test all smoke alarms every month.
- U.S. USFA says to replace a smoke alarm 10 years after its date of manufacture.
- U.S. CPSC says not to install a smoke alarm near a kitchen appliance or a fireplace. This prevents nuisance alarms.
- U.S. CPSC also says to keep alarms away from bathrooms, heating appliances, windows and ceiling fans.
