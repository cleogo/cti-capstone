## Specs
- Product type: Wired interlinked smoke detector (as named on the masterlist)
- Power on the masterlist: 220 V AC with a 9 V battery
- Interlink on the masterlist: Wired

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. Interlinked alarms connect to each other. When one alarm senses smoke, all the alarms sound. U.S. CPSC gives an example. A fire in the basement starts the nearest alarm, and every alarm in the home sounds. Wires usually connect interlinked alarms, but wireless units are also available. UK (Scotland) guidance says that all smoke and heat alarms should be on the ceiling and interlinked.

## Advantages
- U.S. CPSC says that interconnected alarms can give more time to escape and better audibility.
- People hear the alarm in every part of the home, not only near the fire.
- U.S. CPSC says that a wired alarm with a backup battery gives protection also during a power outage.
- U.S. CPSC lists interconnected alarms on house wiring with battery backup in its best level of protection.

## Cautions
- The masterlist name does not state the sensor type. Ask CTI sales for this detail.
- The masterlist name says both "interlinked" and "standalone". Ask CTI sales how the unit links to other alarms.
- U.S. USFA says that only qualified electricians should install hardwired alarms.
- UK (Scotland) guidance also says that a qualified electrician must fit mains-wired alarms.
- U.S. USFA says to replace the backup battery at least once a year. Replace the alarm every 10 years.
- U.S. USFA says to test all smoke alarms every month.
