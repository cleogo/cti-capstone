## Specs

- Model number on the masterlist: SD-426
- Maker: Not named on the masterlist

## Usage
These are general facts for this type of product, not the datasheet of CTI's unit. A smoke alarm sounds when it senses smoke. U.S. USFA says that the two basic types are ionization and photoelectric. Each type detects different kinds of fires. A dual-sensor alarm contains both sensors. U.S. USFA advises alarms inside and outside each bedroom and on every level of the home. Mount the alarm on the ceiling or high on a wall. Obey the instructions of the maker.

## Advantages
- A smoke alarm gives early warning, so people have more time to leave the home.
- A Philippine news report (GMA News) lists the smoke alarm among the items that every home should have.
- U.S. CPSC says that a photoelectric alarm is less likely to give a nuisance alarm during cooking.

## Cautions
- The masterlist does not name the maker of the SD-426. A web search found no single maker for this model number.
- The web search found a Visonic SMD-426, which is a different model number. Do not use its specs for CTI's unit.
- The masterlist name does not state the sensor type or the power source. Ask CTI sales for these details.
- U.S. USFA says to test all smoke alarms every month.
- U.S. USFA says to replace a smoke alarm 10 years after its date of manufacture.
- U.S. USFA says that you can have less than 2 minutes to leave the home after the alarm sounds.
